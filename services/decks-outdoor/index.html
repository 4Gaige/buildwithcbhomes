The backyard should work as hard as the kitchen

Tired deck boards. Unsafe rails. No shade. Outdoor space that never became a real room. Fences and grades that fight how the family actually uses the yard. Those are the problems a deck or outdoor living project is supposed to solve.

Alabama sun, rain, and humidity punish shortcuts on structure and finish. We build for how the space is used, not only how it looks on day one. The fear is a pretty deck that softens early, or a half-finished outdoor kitchen with no weather plan.

We are licensed and insured. Decks and outdoor living are single-job work we do now. Custom homes are a deliberate next step, not the claim this page is making. You can read more about who we are on our About page.

The Job, In Order

How we approach a deck or outdoor living project

01

Walk the yard

We walk the yard and how you gather. Shade, privacy, access, grade, and how the house attaches to the outdoor space all go on the list before anyone talks products.

02

Structure and attachment honesty

Posts, framing, fastening, and waterproofing at the ledger where the job needs it. We say what the structure can carry, not only what the render wants.

03

Scope in writing

You get a written scope before work starts. What we are doing, what it costs, and what weather or access would change. No parts list handed off with a hope that someone coordinates.

04

Protect the house and landscape

We protect the house and the yard as practical. Sequence the work so framing, finish, and weather windows do not fight each other.

05

Punch list, then done

We walk the finished work together, write the punch list, and finish it before we call the job complete.

Structure First

Structure first, then the living layer

Posts, framing, fastening, and finish that matches use. A covered porch, a rebuild, or a new deck all start with structure that will take Alabama weather. The living layer (rails, ceilings, fireplace, seating) sits on that, not instead of it.

Typical scope pieces, always project-specific:

  • New decks and rebuilds
  • Rails, stairs, and covered porches
  • Outdoor living zones and related framing
  • Finish that matches how the space is used

Fence work may be related to a yard job. We do not treat it as a thin second hub here. Materials are discussed on the estimate. We do not invent preferred brands, dollar ranges, or an average Birmingham deck cost. A free estimate is the honest number.

We run the outdoor job as the GC. Structure, attachment, finish, and the trades the plan needs, under one written scope. That is the work. Not a pretty deck that cannot take a Birmingham summer.

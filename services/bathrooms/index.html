The Job, In Order

How we approach a bathroom

01

Walk the wet areas

We walk the bath and how you actually use it. Leak history, ventilation, storage that fails, and the rooms the bath opens into all go on the list before anyone talks fixtures.

02

Diagnose the substrate

When the job needs it, we look at what is behind the tile: pan, board, plumbing, and whether a cosmetic refresh would fail. We would rather say that on the visit than after demo.

03

Scope in writing

You get a written scope before demo. What we are doing, what it costs, and what would change it. Waterproofing is part of that conversation, not a surprise line later.

04

Protect the house

Adjacent rooms get protected. We sequence the wet work so one trade does not wreck the last. A bath remodel should not feel like moving out of your own house.

05

Punch list, then done

We walk the finished work together, write the punch list, and finish it before we call the job complete.

Wet Area, Done Right

What a bathroom remodel usually includes

A cosmetic refresh is new paint and fixtures over a room that still works. A real remodel often includes waterproofing, substrate, plumbing, and ventilation decisions. We say which one you actually have before work starts.

Typical scope pieces, always project-specific:

  • Showers and tubs
  • Tile and waterproofing assemblies
  • Vanities, flooring, and lighting
  • Ventilation as the wet area needs it
  • Plumbing moves the plan requires

Walk-in showers, storage, and accessibility come up on the estimate visit. We do not invent product lines here. We do not publish dollar ranges or an average Birmingham bath cost. A free estimate is the honest number.

We run the whole bath job as the GC. Tile, showers, waterproofing, and the trades the plan needs, under one written scope. That is the work. Not a pretty finish photo with a rushed pan behind it.
